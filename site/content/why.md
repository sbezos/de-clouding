title: "Why"
description: "Five short paragraphs. That's all the philosophy you'll find here."
---

**The models were built from all of us.** Today's AI assistants learned from decades of public writing, photos, code and conversation — most of it posted for other reasons by people who were never asked. Whatever you think about how that happened, the result is a capability that exists because of everybody.

**Part of it is being given back.** Open-weight models — ones you can download and run on your own computer — are that capability, handed out. They are smaller than the hosted giants, but for everyday use they are already good, and they get better every few months.

**The part that isn't given back is you.** When you use a hosted assistant, cloud drive, or smart speaker, the service keeps what you put in. Your questions, files, photos, and the rhythms of your home become the raw material for the next product, on terms the provider can change whenever it likes.

**The inventor's version.** In my day job I tell inventors: if your dataset has value, protect it at all costs. AI is remarkable, but its value is unlocked by access to unique data — control the access and you control the space. A household is a dataset too. Your photos, your messages, the rhythm of your home: that is the unique data, and today it's handed over for free.

**So: keep the gift, close the loop.** Run what you can at home. Where you still need a company's servers — backups, mail delivery, a domain name — pick arrangements where the data is encrypted or portable, so the provider works for you and can be swapped out in an afternoon. This was never about leaving the cloud. It's about deciding what's on it. The [guides](/guides/) do the rest.

---

*One fair question: this site tells you to hand the hard parts to Claude, a hosted assistant. Yes. Use the big tool for the one-time move, then let your own local one take over the day-to-day. Using a ladder doesn't mean living on it.*


{{% deep title="Terms, precisely" %}}
- **"Open-weight" vs "open source."** Most released models publish the trained weights under a license (sometimes with use restrictions) but not the training data or the full training recipe. That is open-*weight*. The Open Source Initiative's definition requires more. The honest term for what you can download and run is open-weight, and that is already enough to own the Model layer.
- **What they were trained on**: web crawls (Common Crawl and its filtered derivatives), code repositories, digitized books, image–caption datasets scraped from the web, and — for the hosted products — user conversations and feedback where terms allow. Whether any of this required permission is being litigated in several countries; the site's claim is narrower and doesn't depend on the outcome: the people who wrote it weren't asked, and the result is being handed back in part.
- **The loop.** A hosted assistant's value compounds from three inputs you supply: your prompts, your documents, and your corrections. Local inference keeps all three. The hybrid keeps the first two.
