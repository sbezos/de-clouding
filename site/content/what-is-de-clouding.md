**De-clouding** (verb: *to de-cloud*) means moving something you rely on (a device, a service, or your data) off a company's servers so it keeps working under your control. That can be hardware in your own home, or rented storage that only ever holds what you've encrypted.

## What counts

- **A device that phoned home now works on your own network.** An energy monitor reflashed with open firmware. A camera that records to a box in the closet. A thermostat that keeps its schedule when the maker's servers are down.
- **A service you rented now runs at home.** Your photo library, password manager, media collection, smart-home brain, or AI assistant.
- **Data that lived in someone else's account now lives in yours,** in formats any tool can open, with an off-site copy the provider can't read.

## What it isn't

- **It isn't leaving the internet.** De-clouded things still reach out, for updates or to a backup bucket that holds only encrypted blobs. What matters is who holds the keys and who can switch it off.
- **It isn't "self-host everything."** Some things are services by nature: the game your kids' friends are in, your bank. There, the move is to set the rules yourself, not to move the service.
- **It isn't only privacy.** It's control: being able to export, move, and keep going when someone else changes the terms. [Why this matters](/why/).

## Three questions

Ask them of anything in your house or on your phone:

1. **If the company disappeared tomorrow, would it still work?**
2. **Can the company read it?**
3. **Can you take it with you, in a format other tools open?**

A thing is de-clouded when the answers are *yes*, *no*, *yes*.
